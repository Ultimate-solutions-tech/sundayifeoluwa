Background

A services business with a capable team and a strong reputation among existing clients, but no deliberate route to new demand. Growth to date had come almost entirely from referrals.

Business challenge

Revenue was unpredictable month to month. Pipeline was invisible, pricing was negotiated from weakness, and the owner carried every serious conversation personally.

Diagnosis

The constraint was not marketing volume but the absence of a defined sales process: no qualification stages, no follow-up standard, and no distinction between enquiries worth pursuing and those that consumed capacity without returning value.

Strategic intervention

Agree the diagnosis with the leadership team, redesign the offer architecture around three clear engagement tiers, define qualification criteria, and install a weekly pipeline review with a single owner for each stage.

Implementation

Sales playbook written and trained in week one; CRM stages rebuilt to match; proposal template and pricing logic standardised; four weeks of deal reviews before the cadence was handed to the internal team.

Measurable results

Placeholder — insert verified metrics with documentation: pipeline value, conversion rate, cycle length, revenue consistency, owner hours recovered.

Lessons learned

Referral dependence feels like a strength until it is measured as a single point of failure. A defined process does not reduce judgement — it gives judgement somewhere to operate.

Background

A product business with a sound offering and positive customer feedback, spending on advertising without a clear view of what produced a sale.

Business challenge

Cost of acquisition rising, conversion inconsistent, and positioning indistinguishable from competitors competing primarily on price.

Diagnosis

Spend was being applied to an undefined audience with an undifferentiated message and no post-click path. The problem was structural, not budgetary.

Strategic intervention

Segment selection and positioning statement, offer and messaging hierarchy, landing path redesign, and a tracking plan that tied spend to qualified demand rather than impressions.

Implementation

Message testing across two segments, weekly review of cost per qualified enquiry, follow-up sequence rebuilt, and a monthly acquisition report owned by the commercial lead.

Measurable results

Placeholder — insert verified metrics with documentation: cost per qualified enquiry, conversion rate, repeat purchase rate, return on spend.

Lessons learned

Acquisition is a system, not a campaign. Without positioning and measurement in place, additional spend accelerates the leak rather than filling the bucket.

Background

An organisation scaling faster than its management layer, with technically strong individuals promoted into people responsibility without preparation.

Business challenge

Decisions bottlenecked at the top, inconsistent performance between teams, and managers reporting low confidence in difficult conversations.

Diagnosis

The gap was not knowledge of the work but the absence of a shared leadership method: no common language for delegation, feedback or decision rights.

Strategic intervention

Competency assessment, a cohort-based curriculum covering judgement, delegation and performance conversations, live practice with real cases, and manager involvement at each stage.

Implementation

Six sessions over twelve weeks, participant application projects between sessions, line-manager check-ins, and a closing review with the executive team.

Measurable results

Placeholder — insert verified measures with documentation: application project outcomes, delegation coverage, engagement or performance indicators, retention.

Lessons learned

Training changes behaviour only when the system around it changes too. Without decision rights and review cadence, new habits decay within a quarter.
